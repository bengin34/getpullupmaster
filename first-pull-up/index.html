How to do your first pull-up

The 5 moves that build rep one·6 min read

If you can't do a single pull-up yet, you don't need more willpower on the bar — you need the right building blocks. These are the five moves the PullUp Master Foundation block is built on, with the doses we start beginners at.

Why most people stay stuck at zero

Jumping up and straining at the bottom of a pull-up once a week doesn't build the strength that gets you over it. A pull-up needs three things you can train separately: grip and shoulder stability to hang, the first movement pattern — pulling your shoulder blades down — and pulling strength through the full range. Train each one directly and the first rep arrives.

The 5 moves

01

Dead hang

Hang from the bar with straight arms and active shoulders. It builds grip and shoulder stability, and develops the connective-tissue base for pulling.

Cue: squeeze the bar, keep your shoulders away from your ears. Work up to a 30-second hang before chasing reps.

Start: 3 sets × 20 seconds
02

Scapular pull

From a dead hang, pull your shoulder blades down and back without bending your arms. Your body rises a few centimetres. This is the very first part of every pull-up.

Cue: think "chest up, shoulders down". Arms stay straight.

Start: 3 sets × 8 reps
03

Negative pull-up

Jump or step up so your chin is over the bar, then lower yourself as slowly as you can. Negatives build the exact strength your first pull-up needs.

Cue: aim for 3–5 seconds down, all the way to straight arms.

Start: 4 sets × 3 reps
04

Australian row

Under a low bar (or rings / a sturdy table), body straight, heels on the floor, pull your chest to the bar. It trains the same muscles with less of your bodyweight.

Cue: body in one line, pull with your elbows. Walk your feet forward to make it harder.

Start: 3 sets × 10 reps
05

Band-assisted pull-up

Loop a resistance band over the bar and under your knee or foot. The band takes some of your weight so you can practise the full movement with clean form.

Cue: start from a full dead hang and finish with your chin clearly over the bar. No band? Do an extra set of negatives instead.

Start: 3 sets × 6 reps

Form rules that save you weeks

  • Full range of motion. A rep counts from a full dead hang (arms straight) to chin clearly over the bar. Half reps feel productive but stall progress — fewer clean reps beat many partial ones.
  • Pull-up vs chin-up. Pull-ups (palms away) hit more lats and mid-back; chin-ups (palms toward you) use more biceps and are usually easier for your first rep. Starting with chin-ups is a legit way in.
  • Pain is a stop sign. Sharp or joint pain in the shoulder or elbow isn't something to push through. Swap to a friendlier movement, go lighter, and see a physio if it persists.

How to progress week to week

Train the five moves two to four times a week with a rest day between sessions. When a movement feels easy two sessions in a row, add a rep or a few seconds. When it feels hard two sessions in a row, take a little off. Every few weeks, test your max: try one strict pull-up from a dead hang.

Once you get your first clean rep, the job changes: turning 1–3 reps into real volume with clusters, negative finishers and chin-over-bar holds — then stacking toward double digits.
