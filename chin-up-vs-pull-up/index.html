Chin-up vs pull-up: what's the difference and which should you do?

Grip, muscles and difficulty compared·5 min read

A pull-up uses an overhand grip (palms away) and a chin-up uses an underhand grip (palms toward you). Chin-ups are usually easier and use the biceps more; pull-ups lean harder on the lats and mid-back.

What is the difference between a chin-up and a pull-up?

It comes down to your hands. For a pull-up, palms face away and hands sit about shoulder-width or a bit wider. For a chin-up, palms face you and hands are typically about shoulder-width. Both start from a dead hang and finish with your chin over the bar. The grip changes how the load is shared across your muscles.

Which muscles do chin-ups and pull-ups work?

Both train the lats, rhomboids, rear delts, forearms and core. The mix differs a little:

  • Pull-up: more emphasis on the lats and mid-back, with the biceps helping.
  • Chin-up: more biceps and lower chest involvement, with the lats still working hard.

For a deeper look, see which muscles pull-ups work.

Is a chin-up easier than a pull-up?

For most people, yes. The underhand grip lets the stronger biceps contribute more and often allows a more comfortable shoulder position, so people typically get more chin-up reps than pull-up reps. That's why chin-ups are a legitimate way to get your first rep. Our first pull-up guide covers the building blocks for either.

Which should you do for your goal?

  • Getting your first rep: start with chin-ups or the grip that feels best, then add the other later.
  • Building a wider-looking back: pull-ups are a staple, but chin-ups still train the lats well.
  • More arm size: chin-ups lean on the biceps more.
  • Sore elbows or wrists: try a neutral grip (palms facing each other) if your bar has handles, or vary your hand width.

Should you train both?

Yes, that's usually the best answer. Rotating grips spreads stress across joints and muscles, and progress in one tends to help the other. A simple approach is to use one grip for your main sets and the other for back-off sets or on alternate sessions.

The big ones are skipping the dead hang, half reps and swinging. We've listed the fixes in pull-up mistakes. And if you're wondering where you stand, check how many pull-ups is good, noting that chin-up counts will usually run higher.

Frequently asked questions

Is a chin-up or pull-up harder?

Pull-ups are harder for most people because the overhand grip uses less bicep help. Chin-ups usually allow more reps.

Do chin-ups build back muscle?

Yes. Chin-ups train the lats and mid-back as well as the biceps, so they're a good back exercise.

Should beginners start with chin-ups or pull-ups?

Many beginners find chin-ups easier, so starting there is a perfectly valid way to get your first rep. You can add overhand pull-ups afterwards.

What is a neutral grip pull-up?

A neutral grip has palms facing each other, using parallel handles. It sits between the two grips and is often comfortable on the wrists and shoulders.
