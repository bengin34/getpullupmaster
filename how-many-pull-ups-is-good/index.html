How many pull-ups is good? Rough standards by level

Rep ranges from beginner to advanced·6 min read

For most adults, one clean strict pull-up is a real milestone, around 5 to 10 is solid, and double digits is strong. These are rough guidelines, not official standards.

How many pull-ups is considered good?

There is no single official number. What counts as good depends on your age, body weight, training history and goals. Still, a rough ladder helps you know where you stand and what to aim for next. Every number below assumes strict reps: a full dead hang at the bottom and chin clearly over the bar at the top, no swinging.

Rough pull-up standards by level

LevelStrict reps in one setWhat it usually means
Starting out0Building hanging strength, negatives and rows
Beginner1–4You've got the movement, now build repeatable volume
Intermediate5–9Solid pulling strength for everyday fitness
Good10–14Double digits; strong for most people
Advanced15+Often ready for weighted pull-ups

Treat these as rough guidelines. Plenty of fit people sit lower, and plenty of lighter or very active people sit higher. Consistency matters more than the label.

What affects how many pull-ups you can do?

  • Body weight and composition. You pull your whole body, so a lighter frame usually has an edge, all else equal.
  • Training history. Back and arm strength builds over months, not days.
  • Limb proportions. Longer arms mean a longer range of motion for every rep.
  • Grip. Hanging strength can be the limiter before your back is.
  • Technique. Clean form wastes less energy. See common pull-up mistakes.

How many pull-ups should a beginner aim for?

If you're at zero, the goal is your first clean rep. Don't worry about a target number yet; follow the progression in our first pull-up guide. Once you have one rep, aim for three to five, then keep stacking toward ten across several weeks.

How do I move up to the next level?

Hitting the next band is mostly about total weekly volume done with good form, not testing your max every day.

  • Train the movement often. Two to four pull sessions a week with rest days works for most people.
  • Use submax sets. Doing several sets that stop short of failure builds more total reps than one grinding set.
  • Add variations. Negatives, band-assisted reps and rows fill in the gaps when strict reps run out.
  • Test occasionally. Every few weeks, try one fresh max set after a rest day.

Do chin-ups count the same as pull-ups?

Chin-ups (palms toward you) usually let you do more reps than pull-ups (palms away). They're a perfectly good strength test, but compare like with like when tracking progress. We break down the difference in chin-up vs pull-up.

Is it worth chasing a big number?

A higher number is nice, but quality and consistency are what keep your shoulders happy and your progress steady. Choose a target that fits your goals, whether that's one rep, ten reps, or adding weight later. Frequently asked questions

Is 10 pull-ups good?

Yes. Ten strict pull-ups, from a dead hang to chin over the bar, is a strong number for most people and a common goal for training.

Is 5 pull-ups good for a beginner?

Five strict pull-ups is a solid result. Many people take months of consistent training to get there from zero.

How many pull-ups should I be able to do?

There's no required number. Rough guidelines: one is a first milestone, 5 to 9 is intermediate, and 10 or more is strong. Your goals and body matter more than any chart.

Do kipping pull-ups count?

For measuring strict strength, no. Count only reps that start from a full hang and finish with your chin clearly over the bar without swinging.
