Pull-up muscles worked: what you're really training

Primary and supporting muscles, by grip·5 min read

Pull-ups mainly work the latissimus dorsi (lats), supported by the mid-back, biceps, rear shoulders, forearms and core. They're a full upper-body pulling exercise, not just an arm move.

What muscles do pull-ups work?

A pull-up moves your whole body weight through space, so many muscles share the job. Some do most of the pulling; others stabilize. Knowing which is which helps you feel the right muscles and spot weak links.

Primary muscles: lats and upper back

  • Latissimus dorsi (lats). The large muscles along your sides and back that pull your arms down and back. They're the main engine of the pull-up.
  • Rhomboids and middle trapezius. They pull the shoulder blades together and help keep them stable.
  • Lower trapezius. It helps pull the shoulder blades down, the first movement of a good rep.
  • Teres major. A small helper that works with the lats.

Secondary muscles: arms and shoulders

  • Biceps and brachialis. They bend the elbow. They work harder with an underhand grip.
  • Brachioradialis. A forearm muscle that assists the elbow bend.
  • Rear deltoids. They help pull the arms back.
  • Forearm flexors. They keep you hanging on to the bar. For many beginners, grip gives out before the back does.

Stabilizers: core and more

Your abs, obliques and glutes keep your body in one line rather than swinging. Your rotator cuff also works quietly to keep the shoulder joint stable. This is a big reason pull-ups feel like a whole-body exercise, and why squeezing your glutes and bracing your core makes reps cleaner.

How does grip change the muscles worked?

GripEmphasis
Overhand, shoulder-width or wider (pull-up)Lats and mid-back lead; biceps assist
Underhand (chin-up)Biceps work harder; lats still strongly involved
Neutral (palms facing)Balanced, often friendly on the wrists and shoulders

How do I feel my back instead of my arms?

If you mostly feel your biceps and forearms, try these cues:

  • Start every rep by pulling your shoulder blades down.
  • Think of driving your elbows toward your back pockets rather than pulling with your hands.
  • Keep your chest tall and avoid shrugging.
  • Use a slow lowering phase to keep tension on the lats.

Form slips are often the cause here, so check common pull-up mistakes.

What other exercises train the same muscles?

If you can't do a pull-up yet, you can still train these muscles. Dead hangs build grip and shoulder stability, scapular pulls teach the first movement, negatives build strength through the range, and Australian rows work the same muscles with less load. All five are laid out in the first pull-up guide.

Frequently asked questions

Do pull-ups work biceps or back?

Both. The lats and mid-back do most of the work, and the biceps assist. An underhand grip shifts a little more load to the biceps.

Do pull-ups work abs?

Your abs and obliques act as stabilizers, keeping your body from swinging. They're working, but they aren't the main target.

Are pull-ups good for your shoulders?

Done with good form and control, pull-ups train the muscles that support the shoulder. If you feel sharp pain, stop and choose a gentler variation.

Why do my forearms give out first?

Grip often lags behind back strength. Dead hangs and longer holds build it up.
