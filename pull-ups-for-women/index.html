Pull-ups for women: how to start and get your first pull-up

The same progression, honest expectations·6 min read

Women can absolutely learn to do pull-ups. The training is the same as for anyone: build grip and shoulder control, practise the movement with hangs, negatives and assisted reps, and add volume gradually. What matters is consistent, structured work, not your sex.

Can women do pull-ups?

Yes. Pull-ups are a strength skill that depends on training, body weight relative to strength, and practice. Many women do not get early exposure to vertical pulling, so they start at zero, but that is a starting point, not a limit. With a sensible progression, a first strict pull-up is a realistic goal for most people who train consistently, and plenty of women go on to multiple reps and weighted sets.

Why is the first pull-up hard, for anyone?

A pull-up asks you to lift your entire body using the back, shoulders and arms. Because it's measured against your own body weight, it is a relative-strength challenge. Two people of different heights, weights and training histories will find it differently hard. That is why advice to just try harder rarely works. Break it into parts you can train: grip and hanging, shoulder-blade control, and pulling strength through the full range.

How do I train for my first pull-up?

The same five building blocks in our first pull-up guide apply. Here they are in the order most beginners use them:

  1. Dead hangs. Build up to a 30-second active hang. See dead hang benefits and progression.
  2. Scapular pulls. From the hang, draw your shoulder blades down without bending your arms.
  3. Australian rows. Pull your chest to a low bar or rings with your body straight. Lower the bar or walk your feet forward to make it harder.
  4. Slow negatives. Step up to the top position and lower yourself for 3-5 seconds.
  5. Band-assisted pull-ups. Practise the full range with part of your weight taken off. See how to use bands.

Train two to four times a week with a rest day between sessions. Add a rep or a few seconds when a movement feels easy two sessions in a row, and ease off when it feels hard twice in a row.

Is it harder for women to do pull-ups?

Average strength differs between groups, and upper-body pulling strength often differs more than lower-body, but averages say little about you. Some people find pull-ups easier because they are lighter, some because they have trained pulling before. The honest answer is that you should expect a plan that fits your starting point, and that's true for anyone. Judge progress against your own numbers, not against a stereotype. If you want to compare, see how many pull-ups is good, remembering these are rough guides.

Do pull-ups change your body or make you bulky?

Training the back, shoulders and arms builds muscle, as any strength training does, but gaining a lot of size takes a long time and a lot of deliberate eating and training. Most people notice mainly that they feel stronger and that everyday pulling, like carrying or climbing, gets easier. Everyone's body responds differently.

Does the menstrual cycle matter for pull-up training?

Some people notice that energy, recovery or motivation fluctuates across their cycle, and some do not. If you notice a pattern, you can plan harder sessions for days when you feel good and use the lighter ones for the rest. It's a personal observation, not a rule. Track how sessions feel and adjust. If you have pain or symptoms that concern you, talk to a doctor.

Chin-up or pull-up first?

Many beginners find the underhand chin-up easier because the biceps help more, so starting there is a legitimate route. You can add overhand pull-ups after. See chin-up vs pull-up to decide which fits your goals.

Form and safety tips

  • Count a rep only from a full hang to chin clearly over the bar.
  • Keep your ribs down and avoid swinging. See the common pull-up mistakes.
  • Treat sharp pain in the shoulder or elbow as a stop sign, not something to push through.
  • Warm up your shoulders before hanging.

Frequently asked questions

Can a beginner woman do a pull-up?

Yes. With a progression of dead hangs, scapular pulls, Australian rows, negatives and band-assisted reps, a first strict pull-up is a realistic goal for most people who train consistently.

How long does it take for a woman to get her first pull-up?

It varies with starting strength, body weight and consistency, and can range from a few weeks to several months. Training two to four times a week with steady progression is the most reliable route.

Are chin-ups easier than pull-ups for women?

For most people of any sex, chin-ups are easier because the biceps assist more. Starting with chin-ups and adding overhand pull-ups later is a good approach.

Will pull-ups make me bulky?

Pull-ups build muscle in the back, shoulders and arms, but gaining a lot of size takes a long time and deliberate training and eating. Most people notice mostly that they get stronger.
