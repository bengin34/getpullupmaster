Pull-up workout plan: a weekly routine to add reps

A sample week plus how to progress·7 min read

To add pull-up reps, train the movement two to four times a week with sets that stop short of failure, mix in negatives and rows, and add a rep or two when sessions feel easy.

How often should you train pull-ups?

Most people progress well with two to four pull sessions a week, with at least a day between hard ones. Pulling strength is a skill as well as a muscle, so frequent, moderate practice usually beats one brutal day. If you can't do a clean rep yet, begin with the first pull-up guide and then return here.

What does a good pull-up workout look like?

A session has three parts:

  1. Warm up: a minute of dead hang and 8 scapular pulls to wake up your shoulders.
  2. Main work: several submaximal sets of strict pull-ups or the closest variation you can do cleanly.
  3. Accessory work: rows, negatives or holds to add volume without grinding.

Sample 3-day pull-up plan for beginners

This week suits someone who can do roughly one to four reps. Rest at least 90 seconds to 2 minutes between sets, and a day or more between sessions (for example Monday, Wednesday, Friday).

DayWork
Day 1Warm-up. 5 sets of about half your max in strict pull-ups. 3 sets × 8–10 Australian rows. 2 × 20-second dead hangs.
Day 2Warm-up. 4 sets × 3 slow negatives (3–5 seconds down). 3 sets of band-assisted reps, 5–6 each. 3 sets × 8 scapular pulls.
Day 3Warm-up. Ladder sets: 1-2-3-2-1 strict reps with short rests. 3 sets × 10 rows. Finish with one chin-over-bar hold.

If your max is zero, replace the strict pull-ups with negatives, banded reps and rows. That is the Foundation approach PullUp Master starts newcomers on.

How do you progress week to week?

  • Easy two sessions in a row? Add a rep to a set, or add one more set.
  • Hard two sessions in a row? Drop a set or take a rep off.
  • Re-test every 3–4 weeks. After a rest day, do one fresh strict max set and update your numbers.
  • Keep form clean. If you swing or cut range, the set is over. See pull-up mistakes for what to watch.

What about more advanced lifters (5+ reps)?

Once you can do several reps, use some of these tools:

  • Volume days: multiple sets at 50–70% of your max reps.
  • Cluster sets: do 2–3 reps, rest 15–20 seconds, repeat, to collect more quality reps.
  • Grease the groove: a few easy sets spread through the day on non-training days, never close to failure.
  • Grip variety: rotate overhand, underhand and neutral. Compare them in chin-up vs pull-up.

How should you recover between pull-up sessions?

Sleep, enough food and a rest day between sessions do more for your numbers than adding extra work. Mild muscle fatigue is normal; sharp joint pain, especially in the elbows or shoulders, means back off and consider seeing a physio. Knowing which muscles pull-ups work helps you tell normal soreness from something worth checking.

Frequently asked questions

How many days a week should I do pull-ups?

Two to four days a week works for most people, with a rest day between hard sessions.

How do I increase my pull-ups fast?

Practice often with submaximal sets, add negatives and rows, keep form strict, and add a rep or a set when sessions feel easy. Rest and sleep also matter.

Can I do pull-ups every day?

Some people do easy sets daily, but daily hard sets usually hurt recovery. If you try it, stay far from failure and stop if your elbows or shoulders complain.

What if I can't do a single pull-up?

Train the building blocks: dead hangs, scapular pulls, negatives, Australian rows and band-assisted reps. They build the strength for your first rep.
